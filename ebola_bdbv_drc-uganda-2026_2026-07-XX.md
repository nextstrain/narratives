# [Overall diversity of BDBV virus](https://nextstrain.org/ebola/bdbv?c=year&d=tree&p=full)


Rapid genomic sequencing by the INRB and partners published the first genomes May 16 within 16 hours of receiving the samples. Since then 138 genomes have been sequenced and shared by INRB.

Sequences from the current outbreak form a novel clade in the BDBV phylogeny which is distantly related to previous outbreak clusters. The outbreak is believed to have started from a zoonitic spill-over event in the Mongbwalu health zone. The phylogenetic placement of sequenced samples is consistent with human-human transmission from a single reservoir source.

# [Mutations](https://nextstrain.org/ebola/bdbv-drc-uganda-2026?d=entropy)

While there is genomic diversity present in the current outbreak, mutations are expected and derive from the error prone replication common in RNA viruses such as BDBV. No mutation in particular appears to be spreading more rapidly than others. The growth of the ourbreak is driven by epidemiological factors not viral adaptation.

# [DRC epi data shows distribution of cases in NE DRC](https://nextstrain.org/staging/ebola/bdbv-drc-uganda-2026-cases)

**This map is drawn from case count reporting via [INRB-UMIE/Ebola_DRC_2026](https://github.com/INRB-UMIE/Ebola_DRC_2026)**

To date there have been over 2,450 confirmed cases in the DRC.
Most of cases have been located within the Ituri province specifically in the Mongbwalu (336), Rwampara (393), and Bunia (507) health zones. Together these locations constitute 64% of all confirmed cases. While these health zones continue to form the epicenter of the outbreak, high levels of transmission have also recently been observed in Nizi.


# [Genome sampling reflects outbreak epi data](https://nextstrain.org/ebola/bdbv-drc-uganda-2026?d=map)

The majority of genomes have come from Bunia (52) and Rwampara (45). While only 9 samples from Mongbwalu have been sequenced, the genomic data confirm these locations as the epicenter of the outbreak.


# [Health zone tree](https://nextstrain.org/ebola/bdbv-drc-uganda-2026?d=tree&f_location=Bunia,Rwampara,Mongbalu&p=full)

While the tree has data from 11 Health Zones, three DRC HZs (Bunia, Rwampara, Mongbalu) contribute 2/3rds of the genomes

The uneven sequencing intensity would bias any phylogenetic estimates of transmission among these three health zones. 

However, the genomic diversity present in these health zones span that of the entire outbreak. 

Samples from other health zones nest within this diversity and suggests transmission chains in these regions ultimately stem from importation from either Bunia, Rwampara, or Mongbwalu.

# [Uganda genomes](https://nextstrain.org/ebola/bdbv-drc-uganda-2026?d=tree&f_country=Uganda&p=full)

There have been 20 confirmed cases in Uganda. Each has been linked to travel from DRC or contact with a known case. The genomics of XX Ugandan samples are consistent with multiple imports from DRC followed by limited local transmission.

As more sequence data becomes available we expect the phylogeographic reconstructions between regions will become more robust and provide insight into the transmission of BDBV within and between health zones.
